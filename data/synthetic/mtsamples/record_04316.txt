Despite their importance, clinical notes are often written by physicians who have limited time and training to learn to write them. Furthermore, the vast majority of clinical notes contain free text, making it difficult for the physician to accurately read and understand the clinical information in the document. There are multiple reasons why the medical literature has not fully utilized the potential of machine learning for clinical notes. First, it is difficult to obtain a large amount of training data because it is not possible to collect clinical notes for every patient. Second, clinical documents are often not available for training, and therefore, the model must be trained on a subset of the data. Third, the domain knowledge is not well defined for clinical text, and the model may need to learn domain knowledge to learn from the document itself. Finally, there is no standard format to describe the clinical notes, and it is therefore difficult to use a shared representation for clinical documents. With the advent of the electronic health record, it has become possible to automatically generate clinical notes using natural language processing (NLP). The goal of this work is to generate a synthetic clinical note using a deep learning approach, and to use this synthetic note to evaluate the model's ability to learn clinical text from a document. < Learning from the clinical document is the first step towards improving the clinical note. As a first step, we generated a synthetic note using the clinical data from the electronic medical record. Then, we evaluated the model on the synthetic note and the clinical documents using a variety of metrics. We found that the model is able to learn the clinical text and make predictions from the synthetic notes. < Hundreds of clinical documents have been manually annotated by physicians.
