A clinical note is usually written by a physician. As such, the content of the notes should be dictated by the physician and should be accurate and complete. It is also important to note the language used to communicate with the patient. In this paper we propose a natural language generation system to generate a clinical note based on the physician's input. Our system is trained to generate notes that have the appropriate structure and content. Our experimental results show that the generated notes have an average F-measure of 0. 8 5 and 0. 6 5 for the sentence level and paragraph level respectively. < 1 0% of the generated clinical notes have the word level accuracy better than the physician. The generated notes also have a similar length to the physician notes. < 2 0% have the sentence and paragraph accuracy better or equal to the physicians. The proposed method is able to generate clinical notes that can be used by other natural language processing systems to help with medical record writing. < Learning to write a clinical document is a difficult task. The difficulty is compounded in the case of medical notes where the physician is not always aware of the words being used by the other authors. In other words, the physician can not always be sure what words are being used in a clinical text. In the case where the physicians are not aware of what words the other researchers are writing, it is difficult to learn to write an accurate clinical note. In order to learn the rules that can identify the words that are being written by the physicians, we propose to use the physician input to learn a rule-based system. Our experiments show that when the physician inputs the text to the learning system, the learned system is able not only to learn what words a physician is writing but also to learn how to write the text. .
