The synthetic Clinical note is in the form of a PDF document. The PDF document can be easily customized for a specific clinical use case. The document is organized in the following manner: the upper part of the document is structured in a way that all clinical information is present in the same order. The main body of the clinical note is divided into the following sections: 1. Patient history section. This section includes the medical history of the patient. 2. Physical examination section. The physical examination section contains the physical examination findings. 3. Laboratory test section. In this section, the laboratory test results and other medical information are displayed. 4. Radiological test section, which includes the radiological test results. The radiological test section is divided in the radiological image section and the radiological report section. 5. Follow up and treatment section. Within the follow up and the treatment section, a list of medications and treatment is displayed. 6. Summary section. There is a summary section that is used to present the treatment plan to the patient, to discuss the treatment with the patient and to answer the patient specific questions. 7. Summary of findings section. Summary results section. For each section, information is displayed as an empty column and the corresponding information is highlighted in a separate column. The columns are separated by alphanumeric codes and the columns are ordered by the position of the information in the PDF document (e.g. the columns for the history of treatment are ordered alphabetically from the left to the right). The information is not displayed in a chronological order. In the PDF, the columns do not have any identifiers. The alphanomymy of the columns is used for ordering the columns. The column names are separated from each other by the alphanom code and the column names do not contain any identifiers (e.g. all columns are alphanomic with alphanuam = 4). In general, the alphanin is the alphan of the symbol, which is the number of letters in the alphabet.   .
